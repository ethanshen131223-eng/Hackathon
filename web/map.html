SPI uses the displayed size range of floating plastic particles. It does not count all marine litter. Sample-based tile estimates are distinct from measured source observations.

Unobserved water is transparent, not pollution-free. Dates, modeled support, and uncertainties are available for each tile.

Coastlines use Natural Earth 1:10 million cartography. Small islands and narrow coastal features may be approximate; this is not a navigation map.

Google Maps provides natural-color basemap imagery. Pollution data, NOAA species ranges, and Protected Planet boundaries are separate sources.

Choose an offshore location on the map or enter coordinates. See how forecast surface currents could move the historical plastic field past that point.

Modeled concentration (pieces/km²)

Model and sources

This scenario starts with the 2014 one-degree microplastics model. It traces water backward using each forecast hour’s current at the selected location, applying the same flow across the nearby area. Estimates stop where the path encounters land, missing baseline coverage, or missing current data.

The scenario excludes new pollution, mixing, settling, and changes in current across space. It illustrates transport of a historical field and does not predict today’s actual plastic concentration.

    How the ranking works

    Environmental priority awards up to 80 points for visible litter density, reaching the cap at 100 items per 100 m. Nearby protected habitat adds 20 points. A dated count from the last 30 days is required.

    Feasibility starts at 100 and subtracts up to 50 points each for disposal travel (capped at 50 km) and the share of budget used. Access and conditions need confirmation before a site is ranked.

    Priority averages both scores equally. These are screening assumptions. Check local requirements and day-of conditions before organizing a cleanup. Ocean SPI is included as context for candidates added from the map.
